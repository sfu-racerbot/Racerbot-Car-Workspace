# Empty-course time trial: aim inside a long view

Compare the old deepest-beam tie-break with the new one in the calibrated, headless F1TENTH simulator. The controller still chooses the same gap; only the target within it can change. The simulator and ROS node use the same aim function. These are not real-car lap times.

## Result: use a 1.24 m minimum band in time trials only

Mean seconds per lap over six seeds (18 laps per car and course). Both arms finished all 60 of their three-lap runs with zero contacts. The same improvement held on the three additional seeds: Spielberg went from 72.658 to 71.483 s for car 1 and 74.036 to 72.850 s for car 2.

| Course | Car 1 before → after (s) | Car 2 before → after (s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Spielberg | 72.614 → 71.508 | 74.068 → 72.850 |
| ASB hallway | 12.717 → 12.717 | 13.192 → 13.192 |
| Race-day course | 10.850 → 10.850 | 10.464 → 10.464 |
| Indoor oval | 8.026 → 8.026 | 8.661 → 8.661 |
| Narrow 1.2 m | 10.222 → 10.222 | 8.885 → 8.885 |

On Spielberg the worst chassis-to-map clearance on car 1 improved from 0.082 to 0.130 m, and stop ticks fell from 35 to 15. Car 2 stayed at 0.130 m and 12 stop ticks. On the four tighter courses, clearance and stop ticks matched the baseline.

**The 12-map floor stress test is not crash-free.** Each arm ran 432 cases. Both had 146 contact runs and no stalls on car 1; both had 13 contact runs and 39 stalls on car 2. No previously successful seed became a contact or stall, and no cell's worst clearance decreased. These inherited failures are why a matrix pass is not clearance to drive the real car.

**Why not 0.62 m?** It also improved the ASB hallway, but on the car 1 October 30 course, race-day floor and 0.85 grip, seed 12345 went from 0.05 m clearance to contact. The cell-level gate alone missed this because another seed improved. Both cars use 1.24 m (four nominal car widths), not a track-specific tuning table.

## Reproduce

From the workspace root, run `bash tools/f1tenth_sim/setup.sh` once. The benchmark uses the current car-specific time-trial settings for both arms. Turn only the new feature off for the baseline. An empty `after:` means the shipped time trial, **not** a normal launch.

```bash
export PYTHONPATH=.sim/python
for seed in 12345 20345; do
  python3 tools/f1tenth_sim/time_trial_benchmark.py --first-seed "$seed" \
    --arm before:open_aim_min_width=0 --arm after: --output ".sim/laps-$seed.json"
done
for car in rb1 rb2; do
  python3 tools/f1tenth_sim/floor_matrix.py --car "$car" --time-trial \
    --arm before:open_aim_min_width=0 --arm after: --track maps \
    --floors car,sfu_course_2026_11_11_rb2 --grip 0.85,1.0,1.15 \
    --seeds 3 --output ".sim/floors-$car.json"
done
```

## Read the evidence

- [Lap runs](2026-11-21-open-aim-laps.csv): three laps per run, including the standing start. Seeds 12345–12347 were used for tuning; 20345–20347 were run separately. Timing is simulated time, not CPU time. Stop steps are individual 25 ms control ticks, not necessarily stalls.
- [Floor matrix](2026-11-21-open-aim-floors.csv): 12 recorded maps × two floor models × three grip levels × two cars. Each row summarizes three seeds; a stall is a contact-free run that did not finish two laps in 90 s. The gate compares contact count, stall count and minimum chassis clearance **per cell**, not averaged over tracks. Clearance slack is 0.03 m.
- [Earlier tuning screen](https://github.com/sfu-racerbot/Racerbot-Car-Workspace/tree/f59b717cb651a3e0ba0751e2ac9841ca7b197d74/tools/run_analysis/results): car 2 steering gain 0.95 and lateral acceleration 3.2 looked fast on five tracks but failed the floor gate in 15 and 16 distinct cells, respectively. Neither is shipped.
